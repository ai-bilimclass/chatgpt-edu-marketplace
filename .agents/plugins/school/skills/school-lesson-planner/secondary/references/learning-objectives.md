## Source rules

Treat a curriculum learning objective and a lesson objective as different fields.

- A **curriculum learning objective** is the exact code and official wording supplied by the teacher or contained in an attached source document.
- A **lesson objective** is a contextualized, observable result for the current lesson, derived from one or more curriculum learning objectives.
- **Assessment criteria** state what features of learner performance will demonstrate the lesson objective.
- **Evidence** is the actual response, product, explanation, performance, or decision the teacher will collect.

Preserve every curriculum-objective code and its wording verbatim. Do not correct, shorten, translate, merge, or paraphrase it silently. If a probable typo or mismatch is detected, quote the supplied wording, flag the issue, and ask the teacher to confirm the authoritative version before creating a new KSP.

Do not search the internet to guess or replace a missing objective. An official source provided by the teacher may be used as the source of the objective. External resources may support lesson content only after the objective is established.

## Scenario-specific requirements

### New KSP

Require exact curriculum-objective codes and official wording. Accept them from the prompt or an attached document; never ask the teacher to re-enter information already present. Stop before final lesson design if the objective is absent or materially ambiguous.

### Review of an existing KSP

Use the objective printed in the KSP as the audit baseline. Report a missing, unclear, duplicated, or misaligned objective as a finding. Do not require the teacher to supply it separately unless the document lacks it.

### Supporting material only

Use the objective from the current conversation or attached lesson plan. Ask for it only when the requested worksheet, cards, rubric, or other material cannot be aligned responsibly without it.

### Reflection or general methodological consultation

Do not block the response merely because no curriculum objective is supplied. Request the objective only if the advice depends on its exact cognitive or subject demand.

## Analysis protocol

For each curriculum learning objective, identify without changing its wording:

1. **Content**: knowledge, concept, procedure, practice, or disposition named in the objective.
2. **Observable action**: what the learner must do.
3. **Cognitive demand**: recall, explain, apply, analyze, evaluate, create, investigate, or another subject-appropriate demand.
4. **Conditions**: source, tool, representation, context, constraints, or level of independence explicitly required.
5. **Evidence**: the minimum learner product or performance that would demonstrate attainment.
6. **Prerequisites and barriers**: prior knowledge, language, representation, pace, motor, sensory, or resource factors likely to affect access.

When several curriculum objectives are provided, determine whether they are integrated, sequential, or too broad for one 40-minute lesson. Preserve all official wording, but recommend prioritization when 40 minutes cannot produce valid evidence for all objectives.

## Deriving lesson objectives

Create one to three lesson objectives unless the lesson genuinely requires more. Each lesson objective must:

- be traceable to a supplied curriculum objective;
- describe learner performance rather than teacher activity;
- use an observable subject-appropriate verb;
- state the relevant content or context;
- be achievable within the fixed 40-minute lesson;
- avoid unsupported percentages, universal claims such as “all learners,” and artificial ability-group labels.

Do not copy the curriculum objective into the lesson-objective field without contextualization. Do not lower its cognitive demand merely to make assessment easier. If scaffolding is required, vary access, support, process, or response format while retaining the common learning demand.

## Assessment alignment

For every lesson objective, create at least one assessment criterion and identify corresponding evidence in a lesson task. Use this chain:

`curriculum objective -> lesson objective -> assessment criterion -> task -> learner evidence -> feedback or teacher response`

Assessment criteria must be concise, observable, and free of activity-only language such as “participates actively.” Descriptors may break a criterion into task-specific steps, but must not introduce a new learning demand absent from the objectives.

## Validation failures

Stop DOCX generation and repair the plan when any of these conditions is true:

- a curriculum objective was invented, paraphrased, or lacks its supplied code;
- a lesson objective cannot be traced to a curriculum objective;
- an assessment criterion has no task or evidence;
- a task assesses a substantially different demand;
- the fixed 40-minute lesson is insufficient for the required evidence;
- a bundled-form document introduces `Жетістік критерийлері`, `Критерии успеха`, `Success criteria`, or an expected-results field not present in that form. Preserve such fields when they belong to a user-supplied template.
